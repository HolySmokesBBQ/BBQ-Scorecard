# CRO audit: holysmokesbbqco.com homepage and the paths into the apps (2026-10-23)

First run of the `cro` skill (Corey Haines marketingskills pack) against the
live site. Scope: the homepage, the four app paths (/scorecard/, /notebook/,
/calculator/, /board/), the three inner pages that feed them (/pit, /episodes,
/about) and the SEO landing pages under /board-cities/ and /calculator-guests/.
The skill's two references (experiments.md, form.md) were read; the site has no
lead or contact form, so form.md yields nothing here.

How it was run: every page fetched live with curl (status, size, headers), the
live HTML diffed against the repo (`website/public-landing/*.html`), the
stylesheet `public/site.css` and the consent script `public/gtm-init.js` read in
full, the four web app entry files (`index.web.html`, `index.notebook.web.html`,
`index.board.web.html`, `index.calculator.html`) and the three onboarding
overlays read from source, and `website/DESIGN.md` read first. Nothing below
recommends against DESIGN.md. No browser was driven, so there are no
screenshots, no GA4 numbers and no mobile render check in this run; layout
calls are reasoned from the CSS and flagged as such.

Every copy line below is a draft. Third-person product copy runs /antislop
before it ships; anything in Joel's first person runs /joel as well. Nothing
here goes live without Joel. Deploys cost Netlify credits, so the fixes are
grouped into three releases at the end.

## What the page is and what it is for

- **Page type:** brand homepage that doubles as the only landing page for the
  apps. No pricing page, no signup form, no demo.
- **Primary conversion goal:** not defined anywhere, and that is finding #1.
  Four equal cards each carry a Launch button plus store badges, and the hero
  CTAs are a scroll ("See the apps") and a page hop ("Watch the latest cook").
  Nothing on the page is measured as a conversion.
- **Traffic context (per DESIGN.md):** phones, arriving from a store listing or
  a search. The SEO pages (/board-cities/, /calculator-guests/) carry their
  own CTAs straight into the apps with presets (`/board/?city=`,
  `/calculator/?g=`), which is good; the homepage card buttons carry nothing.
- **Verified fact base:** live homepage is byte-identical to the repo except
  the footer, where the live build uses extensionless links (`/privacy`) and
  the repo has `/privacy.html`. Both resolve 200. Noted for the Website
  session so the next deploy does not look like a regression either way.

## Quick wins (one release, no design changes)

1. **Fix the `#apps` anchor so the heading is visible after the jump.**
   - Current: `id="apps"` sits on the `<nav class="apps-grid">`, below the
     `THE APPS` h2. "See the apps", the nav item THE APPS and the skip link
     all land with the heading above the viewport, so the visitor arrives
     mid-grid with no label.
   - Draft: move the id to the enclosing `<section>`, or keep it and add
     `scroll-margin-top` sized to the heading. CSS only; zero copy.
   - Why: the primary CTA's one job is to deliver the visitor to a labelled
     destination. Right now it delivers them to the middle of a card row.
2. **Add install referrers to the three Play links.**
   - Current: `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.holysmokesbbq.scorecard`
   - Draft: append `&referrer=utm_source%3Dholysmokesbbqco.com%26utm_medium%3Dwebsite%26utm_campaign%3Dhomepage`
     (same pattern for notebook and board, and a `utm_campaign=board-cities`
     variant on the SEO pages).
   - Why: Play Console then attributes installs to the website by page. It is
     Google-side attribution, so it adds nothing to what the site collects and
     needs no consent text change. For the App Store, the same needs Joel's
     App Analytics provider token (`pt=`), then `ct=homepage` on the link.
3. **Say what the iPhone version costs, next to the badge.**
   - Current: the Scorecard card shows "Download on the App Store" under a
     page titled "Free BBQ Apps" and a schema block that says price 0.
   - Draft (verify the current iOS build's model with the Scorecard session
     first): "iPhone: the first 5 reviews are free, then one $3.99 unlock.
     Web and Android are free."
   - Why: a buyer who meets the paywall after reading "free" reads it as
     bait. The ASO audit found the same gap on the store listing itself.
     Saying the price on the page before the tap is the fix on this side.
4. **One account line per card.**
   - Current: nothing on the page says whether sign-in is needed. In the code,
     all four work without an account; sign-in is only for sync (Scorecard,
     Notebook) or for submitting a price (Board); the Calculator has no auth.
   - Drafts: Scorecard and Notebook: "No account needed. Sign in only to sync
     across devices." Board: "Browse prices without an account. Sign in to
     submit one." Calculator: "No account, no install. It runs here."
   - Why: "will this make me sign up?" is the top objection for a free tool
     and the truthful answer is the best one available. These are present
     tense facts about the code, not promises about the future.
5. **Correct the Board numbers and keep them from drifting.**
   - Current: the card says "Covering 100+ cities across 23 states."
     `website/src/board/schema.js` has 109 cities and 24 distinct state codes.
     The /board/ pre-render paragraph still says "Launched with coverage in"
     four cities and lists eight. Three different numbers for one app.
   - Draft: "109 cities in 24 states, and growing as people submit prices."
     (verify the count the day it ships) and rewrite the /board/ pre-render
     list from the same schema so the two never disagree again.
   - Why: DESIGN.md's first Do is "verify every number against the code." A
     visitor who opens Board from a city that is not on the eight-city list
     has just been told two different stories.
6. **Fix the Scorecard web page description.**
   - Current (`index.web.html`): "Score BBQ restaurants on 10 competition-style
     categories. Log every cook with rubs, woods, temps, and weather. Compare
     with friends." The middle sentence is Notebook's feature.
   - Draft: "Score every BBQ joint you visit on 10 competition-style
     categories. Rank them, compare with friends, and find the best BBQ near
     you."
   - Why: this is the text under the link in search results and on shared
     cards. A searcher who wants a cook log clicks, finds a restaurant scorer
     and leaves.
7. **Take the em dashes out of the app page titles.**
   - Current: `/calculator/` and `/board/` titles and og:titles use an em dash
     ("BBQ Board — Local Butcher & Meat Prices"). DESIGN.md: no em dashes
     anywhere in copy. These strings show in browser tabs, search results and
     share cards.
   - Drafts: "BBQ Meat Calculator: How Much Meat and Cook Time | Holy Smokes
     BBQ" and "BBQ Board: Local Butcher and Meat Prices | Holy Smokes BBQ".
   - Why: consistency with the rule, and the colon reads cleaner in a 60
     character SERP title.
8. **Serve the four card logos as AVIF or WebP.**
   - Current: four PNGs, 130 KB together, the heaviest non-photo assets on the
     page. The brand logo already ships AVIF and WebP with a PNG fallback.
   - Draft: same `<picture>` pattern; expect about 30 KB total.
   - Why: the cards are the conversion surface, and on a phone from a store
     listing every 100 KB before the Launch button paints is a cost.

## High-impact changes (bigger, prioritize)

**A. Define the conversion and measure it.** Right now there is no way to
know which card, button or CTA earns a tap. Plan: GA4 events on the homepage
tag only, fixed labels, no free text: `app_launch_click {app}`,
`store_badge_click {app, store}`, `hero_cta_click {target}`,
`more_card_click {card}`. Mark `app_launch_click` and `store_badge_click` as
key events. House rule: the consent card text is built from every `track()`
parameter, so this adds four events and three parameter names to the sender
inventory, the privacy policy and the card text, and bumps
`CONSENT_TEXT_VERSION` (which re-asks visitors in the regions that see the
banner). Route through the Security auditor before building. Note the funnel
split: the homepage reports to G-7X235L8GQS and each app to its own property,
so "landed on /, opened Scorecard" can only be seen as a click event on the
homepage side. The events are that bridge.

**B. Product imagery on the cards.** Four centered paragraphs are the whole
pitch; nothing shows the product. Reuse the existing store screenshots (Joel
makes these; do not generate new ones) at one per card, framed in the card's
color border, sized the way the lead cards size logos. Test it (T5 below)
rather than assuming: on a dark menu board a phone frame may fight the system.

**C. A proof line the code can back.** With zero store ratings there are no
quotes to show yet. The honest proof is in the data: 109 cities, 11
thermometer apps imported, 10 scoring categories, 45 photos, 3 episodes.
One line under THE APPS heading, numbers pulled at build time where possible.
Draft: "109 cities of meat prices. 11 thermometer apps imported. 10 scoring
categories. All of it free on the web." (verify each number the day it ships).
When the review prompt the ASO audit queued yields the first real ratings,
the first two short quotes replace this line.

**D. A router line for "which one is for me?".** Four apps and a calculator
is a decision tax at the exact moment the visitor is deciding. One line, high
in THE APPS band. Draft: "Eating out: Scorecard. Cooking at home: Notebook.
Buying meat: Board. Feeding a crowd: Calculator." (third person, /antislop).

**E. Board lands on one default city for everyone.** `App.board.jsx` opens on
`milwaukee_wi` unless localStorage says otherwise, and the first onboarding
card says "Pick your city" but does not let you pick it. A visitor from
Austin who tapped Launch Board on the homepage sees another city's prices
first. The homepage cannot know the city, so this is a Board session item,
routed through the auditor: either a city picker on onboarding card one, or
an opt-in location prompt there. The /board-cities/ SEO pages already pass
`?city=`, so the pattern exists.

**F. The two SPA shells show nothing until the bundle runs.** Pre-JS,
/notebook/ renders the words "BBQ NOTEBOOK" and /scorecard/ a logo
placeholder; neither has a `<noscript>` line or a link back to the site.
/board/ and /calculator/ both have a pre-render paragraph and a brand link
(Calculator tracks it as `cross_app_nav`). Give Scorecard and Notebook the
same: one sentence of what the app is, one "Holy Smokes BBQ Co" link in the
header or footer. Cheap cross-app discovery, and a visitor whose bundle fails
(the site already tracks ChunkLoadError) still sees a page.

**G. "Watch the latest cook" is two hops.** The hero button goes to
/episodes, where the visitor picks again and then leaves for YouTube. Also
"latest" is ambiguous there: Episode 2 carries an October 10 date and sits
below Episode 3 (September 26). Candidate: link the button straight to the
newest video and let /episodes be the catalogue (T7). It sends the visitor
off-site in one tap, which is exactly what the button promises.

## Test ideas

Traffic is small (the analytics audit the same day found single-digit users
on a hidden page; the homepage number was not read this run). Classic A/B
splits will not reach significance for months. Run these as before/after
windows of four weeks each in GA4 once the events in A exist, one change per
window, and read direction, not p-values. Use the `ab-testing` skill for the
design when traffic justifies a true split.

The hero sub line ("Three BBQ apps and a meat calculator, built by a Pastor
and Pitmaster who grew up in Kansas City.") is Joel's wording and is not a
test subject. The tests below change what sits around it.

| # | Test | Hypothesis | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Add the router line (D) directly under the hero sub | Naming the use case lifts taps on the matching card, especially Notebook and Calculator which sit in the second row | app_launch_click per card |
| T2 | Hero CTA pair: "See the apps" + "Watch the latest cook" vs "Launch Scorecard" + "See the apps" | A named primary action converts more than a scroll; DESIGN.md already prefers naming the app | hero_cta_click, app_launch_click |
| T3 | Hero `min-height` 86vh vs about 72vh | Letting the top edge of the first card show above the fold is a scroll cue; fewer visitors leave from the hero without scrolling | scroll depth to #apps, exits from / |
| T4 | Lead row order: Scorecard then Board vs Board then Scorecard | The newest app with the clearest question ("Where's the cheapest brisket?") may earn the first tap | app_launch_click split |
| T5 | Card body: current 50 to 60 words vs about 25 words plus one screenshot (B) | Scanners tap what they can see; readers were already converting | app_launch_click, time to first click |
| T6 | Store badges above Launch vs below | Depends on the goal chosen in A: if installs matter more than web sessions, badges go first | store_badge_click vs app_launch_click |
| T7 | "Watch the latest cook" to /episodes vs straight to the newest video | One tap beats two; the catalogue page keeps its own nav entry | outbound clicks to youtu.be per session |
| T8 | Proof line (C) present vs absent | Concrete numbers raise trust on a page with no ratings | app_launch_click, store_badge_click |
| T9 | Calculator card last sentence kept vs cut | "If you're feeding twenty, you don't need an app for that" is honest and funny, but /calculator-guests/for-20-people.html exists and sends 20-guest searchers to the same tool | Calculator launch rate |

## Already right

- The page is light and honest: about 380 KB with fonts and the hero photo,
  AVIF hero with preload, no third-party scripts before consent where consent
  is required, no popups, no fake urgency, no "Get started" buttons.
- The consent banner is display-only where the law needs it, has equal-weight
  buttons and leaves the page fully usable. The footer "Analytics choices"
  link makes withdrawal real. The banner's colors (`#1f1f1f`, `#d4a64a`) are
  slightly off the DESIGN.md palette; cosmetic, fix when the text next
  changes.
- Every control is 44px or taller, the skip link works, store badges only
  appear for stores the app is on, and the "Coming soon to the App Store"
  badge is a non-link so it never promises a page that is not there.
- Launch buttons name the app. The 2+2 grid with a heavier lead row gives
  the page a hierarchy even without a single primary CTA.
- The SEO landing pages do the thing the homepage does not: a preset CTA
  straight into the tool ("Open the Calculator, preset for 10 guests").
- Joel's story on /about and the photos on /pit are the trust layer, and they
  are the right kind: specific, his words, real pictures.

## Limitations

- No GA4 read this run: the browser pane was off-limits to this agent, so
  current homepage users, scroll depth, device split and exit rate are
  unknown. The next `holy-smokes-mwf` Wednesday briefing can fill these in.
- No render check at 375px. The hero stacks nav, a 112px logo, an h1 that
  wraps to two or three lines at 60px, the tagline, the sub and two buttons
  inside `min-height: 86vh`; on a short phone the buttons may sit below the
  first screen. T3 is the test; a screenshot from the Website session settles
  it faster.
- The iOS price model in quick win 3 is taken from the ASO audit's reading of
  the listing and must be confirmed against the shipped 4.4.x build.
- No heatmaps or session recordings exist for the site, and adding a tool for
  them would widen the consent inventory. Not recommended at this traffic.
- /yourpit/ is out of scope by instruction.
